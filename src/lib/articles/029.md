## Event Overview

On November 15, 2026, the Bay Area Founders Club held its sixth Top AI Researchers Roundtable and BBQ at a private residence in Los Altos Hills. The gathering consisted of a discussion about AI infrastructure and development with a barbecue reception, creating opportunities for professional exchange and informal networking. Volunteers from the Silicon Valley Youth Entrepreneurship Program (SVYEP) supported the event through setup, guest and speaker check in, and cleanup.

The roundtable was designed to encourage direct conversation among researchers from frontier AI labs, including OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, and xAI. Its small, private format provided a setting for exploring unresolved questions about the computing resources and technical improvements needed to support increasingly capable AI systems.

## Exploring AI Infrastructure and Development

The event's agenda focused on questions about the future of AI, particularly how the infrastructure behind AI systems could support continued progress. Two central questions were whether obtaining more computing power or making better use of existing resources would have a greater effect on model development and how effectively the computing power available to AI labs can lead to improvements in their models.

This question reflects the relationship between AI's growing capabilities and the systems needed to support them. Training models, generating responses, and running AI agents can place different demands on computing resources. As agents perform longer tasks, use external tools, and run code, researchers must consider how infrastructure can adapt to accommodate these changing needs.

These questions connected to another question discussed during the roundtable: how hardware influences the way AI models are built. Different chips offer different strengths in performance, memory, and software support. The roundtable raised questions about the challenges of moving models between hardware platforms and how closely model design should be shaped by the equipment available.

Another theme of the event examined the role of AI in developing future AI systems. The agenda considered how AI could help researchers write code, analyze results, and conduct experiments. It also asked how researchers could determine whether these tools were producing sustained advances in AI development.

The final core topic considered how progress could continue when access to additional computing resources becomes limited. The agenda discussed how power availability, networking, memory, and construction timelines could be factors affecting expansion. Together, these questions examine both the resources AI requires and the improvements that could help researchers use them more effectively.

## SVYEP Volunteers Behind the Scenes

Before the roundtable began, SVYEP volunteers helped the host prepare for the gathering. They prepared name tags, checked in attendees as they arrived, and helped direct parking. These tasks helped create an organized and welcoming environment for the afternoon.

Supporting the event gave volunteers practical experience with the coordination and communication involved in hosting a professional gathering. Preparing materials and welcoming guests required attention to detail and teamwork, while also giving students an active role in Silicon Valley's technology community. Their contributions supported the setting in which researchers and other professionals could meet and exchange ideas. SVYEP volunteers were also given the chance to connect and socialize with professionals working on the frontier of AI.

## Building Connections Over the BBQ

<img src={images[4]} alt="Two people tending skewers on the grill during the barbecue" width="1328" height="988" loading="lazy" class="mx-auto mb-10 h-auto w-full max-w-[600px] rounded-3xl" />

The event concluded with a barbecue where professionals and SVYEP volunteers could connect in a more informal setting. Sharing a meal created opportunities for introductions and conversations, bringing students into contact with people working in technology, research, and entrepreneurship.

For SVYEP volunteers, the afternoon combined community service with opportunities to build professional connections. After helping prepare the event and welcome attendees, students could participate in the social gathering they had helped make possible. The experience demonstrated how volunteering can give young people both practical responsibilities and access to a broader community as they explore their own interests and future paths.
